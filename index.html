What it does for a working lab

Built around the questions a bench scientist actually asks: where is it, is it still good, and what did we do to it last.

Every vial has an address

Freezer and box are required for every new strain, with rack and position optional. The storage map lists each box, warns when two vials claim the same spot, and lists strains that still have no location.

Revival dates that warn you early

Set a revival interval and the landing page lists strains that are due or overdue, with an amber warning five days ahead. Each check is dated and carries the name of the person who logged it. A failed revival keeps the strain in red until a successful check is recorded.

See the plate in 3D

Every strain gets a plate built from its record, with colonies at their typical size and shape: smooth domes, bead-like heaps or branching arms. Upload a photograph of your own plate and it becomes a detailed relief of that exact dish, with each colony raised from the agar and the agar keeping its colour from the photo.

Turn it, zoom in, switch between a dark and a white background, or download it as an STL file for 3D printing. The printed plate matches the one on screen.

Bring your spreadsheet

Upload an Excel or CSV list with your own column names. You see a preview first, rows with problems are listed by row number, and strains already in the catalogue are flagged so nothing is added twice.

QR labels for vials

Print labels with a QR code that opens the strain's record on any phone, plus its ID, name and storage position.

Works on phones and tablets

Scan a code on the Connect page and use the same live library on your own device. No app store, nothing to install.

One lab, one installation

Each lab runs its own copy on its own computer or server. New colleagues ask to join with the lab's code, and a lab admin approves them.

Your data, always exportable

A lab administrator can download a ZIP of plain CSV files with everything in the lab's catalogue.

Your data stays with you

Where it lives

The library runs on a computer or server that you or your organisation control. Strain records, photographs and models are stored there, in one data folder that is easy to back up. The authors never receive your data, and the site you are reading has no sign-in and holds none.

Nothing needs an internet connection beyond your own network. Optional AI helpers are off unless the lab adds its own key.

Who is responsible

Whoever runs an installation is responsible for its security, backups and the people it gives access to. Anyone who can reach the server can open its files, so keep it where only trusted people have access. The software is shared under the MIT licence, as it is, without warranty.

Questions

Is it free?

Yes. It is released under the MIT licence, so labs can use, change and share it at no charge. The code will be published on GitHub, with each release archived on Zenodo under a citable DOI.

Can I try it now?

Watch the tour above to see every main screen. A public demo with example strains and plate photographs is on the way. It will reset itself every few hours, so nothing entered there is kept. Write to us if your lab would like to be an early user.

What does it run on?

Python on Windows, Mac or Linux, with a web browser on any device on the same network. A Docker setup is included and is built by the automated tests on every change.

Can several people use it?

Yes. Each person has their own account, and everyone works on the same live catalogue from their own computer, tablet or phone. One installation holds one lab. A second lab sets up its own installation and keeps its data to itself.

Is it suitable for regulated work?

Every entry is dated and tied to the account that made it. Nothing is deleted: entries are voided with a reason, and every change goes into an audit log. Electronic signatures and formal GLP or GMP validation are not part of this release, so check with your quality team before using it for regulated records.

How do I cite it?

A paper describing the tool is in preparation. Until it is published, please cite the software release; its DOI will appear here once version 4.7.0 is archived on Zenodo.
